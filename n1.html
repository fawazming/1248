PART A - Condensed Notes (Keyword-Focused)

A1. Introduction to Research [Lecture 1]

Definition: Research is an organized and systematic way of finding answers to questions - an original investigation undertaken in order to contribute to knowledge and understanding in a particular field.

What is NOT research (favourite exam list)

  • Mere information gathering / discovery; transportation or transcription of facts (no interpretation).
  • Rummaging for information (e.g. a "house for sale" sign).
  • Preparation of teaching materials; provision of advice/opinion (consulting); feasibility studies.
  • Routine data collection; routine information-system development.
  • Playing with technology; book report; programming project; doing what others already did.

Note: Each of these can be done as part of research - the essence of research is the interpretation of data and the generation of new knowledge.

Key components of research

A question of interest to the community

A claim (the contribution)

Evidence (data gathered)

Argument - links evidence to claim

Research characteristics

Remember:Controlled - Rigorous - Systematic - Valid - Empirical - Critical. (Mnemonic: CR-SVEC)

Types of research

By ApplicationBy ObjectivesBy Mode of Inquiry
Basic - AppliedDescriptive - Exploratory - Correlational - ExplanatoryQuantitative - Qualitative - Mixed

Research philosophy / paradigm

Positivist - quantitative; measuring & counting without bias.

Naturalist / Interpretivist / Constructionist - qualitative; observation, questioning, description.

Sources of research problems - the 4 Ps

  • People - research involves a group of people.
  • Problems - to address an issue.
  • Programs - effect of an intervention (e.g. a new computer system).
  • Phenomena - to establish the existence of a regularity.

Considerations in selecting a research problem

List:Interest - Magnitude (narrow & manageable) - Measurement of concepts - Level of expertise - Relevance - Availability of data.
Avoid dead topics (most recent refs>=10 yrs) and dying topics (closely related refs>=5 yrs).

High-quality research / typical risks

Good research requires: clear scope & limitations; a reproducible & verifiable process; objective design; high ethical standards; documented limitations; adequate data analysis; unambiguous findings justified by evidence.

Typical risks: solving the wrong problem; moving target; data-collection difficulty; overlooking previous work; being blindsided by competitors; misinterpreting results; contravening research conventions; outside interruptions.

A2. Research Process & Proposal Writing [Lecture 2]

4 Stages:1. Determine what type of research to do ->2. Plan how to do it ->3. Do the research ->4. Deliver the output.

Literature Review -> Problem Statement -> Research Objectives -> Research Design -> Research Methodology -> Research Activity -> Result -> Conclusions

Items 1-5 form the Research Proposal; the last node is the actual research. The workflow is iterative (feedback loops).

Standard Research Proposal contents

Example 1 (academic)Example 2 (funding)
Title - Introduction - Literature Review - Research Methodology - Research DesignTitle - Background (incl. literature search) - Aims & objectives - Methodology/methods - Timetable/Gantt chart - Budget & resources - Dissemination

Research Design options

DesignSteps / notes
Survey - QuantitativeResearch questions -> statistical hypothesis -> instrument -> pilot test (validity) -> data collection -> population/sample -> statistical analysis -> report.
Survey - QualitativeQuestions -> instrument -> pilot test -> collection (interview/observation/focus group) -> sample -> qualitative analysis -> report.
ExperimentDetermine variables -> steps -> control experiment -> run experiment & control -> analysis -> report.
Prototype DevelopmentProblem analysis -> software design -> implementation (rapid prototyping, components) -> testing -> analyse -> report.
Formal MethodsProblem analysis -> choose formal model (analytic/stochastic/computational) -> develop model -> analyse (theorem proving/model checking) -> report.
Library ResearchResearch questions -> search materials -> analyse findings -> report.

Gantt: Indicate activities, milestones and time frame. Resources needed: manpower, finance, hardware, software, data, samples.

Dissemination: Thesis - journal papers - conference papers - oral presentations - IPR.

Why proposals fail / are rejected

  • Not a research project - not new, development (not research), or not feasible.
  • Aims, objectives, issues, research gap unclear or vague.
  • Mismatch between approach and the issues to be addressed.
  • Plan too ambitious / unrealistic timescale; insufficient background research.
  • Data-collection or data-analysis method insufficiently detailed; poor budget; topic over-done.

Outcomes of evaluation: Accepted - Accepted with modification - Resubmit - Reject.

A3. Problem Formulation & Research Objectives [Lecture 3]

Definition:Problem formulation is usually one sentence stating what research problem you aim to address and to whom and where it is relevant. It is the heart/core of the thesis, developed iteratively (in interaction with the supervisor).

Characteristics of a good research topic

Remember:Interesting - Researchable - Significant - Manageable - Ethical.

MSc vs PhD research

MScPhD
To solve a problem using the methodology you have learned.Using the methodology and experience of solving problems to produce something new.

Statement of the problem - two forms

Statement form -> Hypothesis

Research Question form -> Question

Hypothesis: A tentative prediction / educated guess about the relationship between two or more variables. A research question is simply a hypothesis stated in question form.

Types of hypothesisMeaning
Null (H0)States there is no relationship / no difference. Never "proven"; either rejected or not.
Experimental / Alternative (H1, H)Prediction that there will be significant findings. Directional (one-way) or non-directional (two-way).

Characteristics: Simple, specific & conceptually clear - capable of verification - related to existing body of knowledge - operationalisable (measurable).

Functions: Gives focus - tells you what data to collect - enhances objectivity - adds to theory formulation.

Writing with IF/THEN: If [independent variable] is related to [dependent variable], then [prediction].
e.g. "If water is related to plant growth, then the more you water plants, the bigger they will grow."

Research Objectives

  • Definition: a description of what is to be achieved by the study. Importance: narrows/focuses the study, guides data collection, facilitates the methodology.
  • Main objective - one per study, general, uses action verb, shows target population/place.
  • Specific objectives - several, describe the variables measured, use action verbs (determine, find out, identify).

SMART:Specific - Measurable - Achievable - Realistic - Time-bound. Also: logical sequence, clear & unambiguous, addressing one thing only.
