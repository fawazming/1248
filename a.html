Suggested text to insert (adapt with your own words)

Executive Summary (sample): This study proposes an Explainable Federated Deep Learning framework for privacy-preserving disease prediction in Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) healthcare systems. Centralised deep learning achieves high accuracy but exposes sensitive patient data; Federated Learning protects privacy but yields opaque models; and existing XAI-integrated FL studies evaluate these dimensions only in isolation. Adopting Design Science Research, the study will design and implement a six-layer architecture integrating FL, DL and XAI (SHAP/LIME), then evaluate it on public healthcare datasets across accuracy, privacy preservation, explainability and communication efficiency, comparing it with existing models. The expected outcome is a unified, trustworthy and clinically adoptable framework.

Scope & Limitation (sample):Scope: the study focuses on disease prediction from IoMT-derived physiological data using FL-trained deep models with post-hoc explanations; it covers selected public datasets and evaluates four dimensions (accuracy, privacy, explainability, efficiency). Limitation: experiments use simulated federated clients rather than live hospital deployment; evaluation depends on the availability and quality of public datasets; explanation quality is assessed via proxy fidelity metrics; and real-world clinical validation is outside the study's timeline and resources.
