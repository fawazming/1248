Appendix | Wider Course Concepts

Extra background from the CSC 713 lecture material, useful for essay-style answers.

AI, agents and research areas

AI (machine intelligence) is intelligence demonstrated by machines, in contrast to the natural intelligence of humans and animals. In computer science, AI research is the study of intelligent agents: any device that perceives its environment and takes actions to maximise its chance of achieving its goals.

Key research areas: problem solving, planning and search; knowledge representation; automated reasoning / inference; machine learning; natural language processing; computer vision; robotics.

An agent perceives its environment through sensors and acts through actuators. A rational agent selects the action expected to maximise its performance measure, given the evidence and its built-in knowledge.

Knowledge representation

The main ways knowledge is represented for an AI program:

  • Expert / rule-based systems - knowledge bases of hundreds or thousands of IF (condition) THEN (action) rules, usually gathered from experts. Rules can chain and fire off other rules.
  • Semantic networks - a graph that represents relationships and word descriptions between objects.
  • Decision / search trees - tree views of algorithms and game paths.
  • Neural networks - a computer version of the brain's neurons.

Artificial Neural Networks

An ANN is a collection of connected units (artificial neurons) that loosely model biological neurons. Each connection transmits a weighted signal. An artificial neuron has many inputs and one output; if the sum of weighted inputs is strong enough, it fires. Training is the process of adjusting the weights and threshold values.

Types: feed-forward Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), LSTM/GRU, Self-Organizing Map (SOM), and deep networks.

Machine learning and deep learning

Machine learning (Arthur Samuel, 1959) is "the ability to learn without being explicitly programmed". It helps a computer learn and evolve as it is exposed to new data. Deep learning studies deep neural networks and emulates the inner layers of the brain, creating knowledge from multiple layers of information processing - strong for image recognition, sound recognition and recommender systems.

AI versus AI (GAN)

A Generative Adversarial Network (Ian Goodfellow, 2014) has two neural networks: a generator that produces a fake output, and a discriminator that compares it with real data and feeds back how realistic it is. This is the "AI versus AI" idea used in Question 2's BOT evaluation.
