PART D - Likely Exam Questions (with Model Answers)

These are drawn from the tutorial paper, the assignment brief and the lecture learning outcomes. Answer in Define -> List -> Explain -> Example format. 1. Define research and state five characteristics of research.Research is an organized and systematic way of finding answers to questions; an original investigation that contributes to knowledge and understanding. Characteristics: controlled, rigorous, systematic, valid, empirical, critical. 2. Explain any five activities that are commonly mistaken for research.Information gathering/discovery; transportation/transcription of facts; preparation of teaching materials; provision of advice/opinion (consulting); feasibility studies; routine data collection; routine information-system development; playing with technology; book report; programming project. (Explain each briefly - see Part B Q2b.) 3. Itemize and explain the stages of the research process using a diagram.Literature Review -> Problem Statement -> Research Objectives -> Research Design -> Research Methodology -> Research Activity -> Research Result -> Conclusions. Four activity stages: determine type, plan, do, deliver. The process is iterative. (Draw the diagram from Part B Q2a.) 4. What is a research problem? State the characteristics of a good research problem.A research problem is the issue/gap a study addresses, usually expressed in one sentence stating what is to be addressed and to whom/where it is relevant. Characteristics: interesting, researchable, significant, manageable, ethical (plus clear, novel, feasible, relevant - FINER). 5. Distinguish between a research question and a hypothesis, and explain the types of hypothesis.A research question is a hypothesis in question form; a hypothesis is a tentative prediction about the relationship between two or more variables. Types: null (H0) - no relationship/difference; alternative/experimental (H1) - directional or non-directional. 6. Explain the characteristics and functions of a hypothesis.Characteristics: simple, specific, conceptually clear; capable of verification; related to existing body of knowledge; operationalisable. Functions: focus, guides data collection, enhances objectivity, adds to theory. 7. What are research objectives? Explain the SMART qualities of specific objectives.Objectives describe what the study will achieve; they narrow the study, guide data collection and facilitate the methodology. Qualities: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound; also logical, clear, unambiguous, addressing one thing only. 8. Differentiate between MSc and PhD research.MSc solves a problem using methodology already learned; PhD uses methodology and problem-solving experience to produce something new. 9. Discuss the sources of research problems and the considerations in selecting one.Sources: the 4 Ps - People, Problems, Programs, Phenomena. Considerations: interest, magnitude, measurement of concepts, level of expertise, relevance, availability of data; avoid dead/dying topics. 10. What are the contents of a standard research proposal? Why do proposals fail?Contents: Title; Background (incl. literature search); Aims & objectives; Methodology/methods; Timetable/Gantt chart; Budget & resources; Dissemination. Failures: unclear aims/gap, approach-issue mismatch, over-ambition, insufficient background, insufficient methodological detail, unrealistic timescale, poor budget, over-done topic. 11. What is a literature review, and what should it do?A systematic, comprehensive and critical method for identifying, evaluating and interpreting published work related to your topic. It compares/contrasts authors, groups similar conclusions, criticises methodology, notes disagreements, highlights gaps, and shows how your study relates to the literature. 12. Using relevant criteria, explain how you would critically evaluate a journal article.Use Relevancy, Objectivity, Reliability, Accuracy, Currency, Legitimacy (CRVEC-L) - explain each and state which are most critical for your topic. 13. "Intensive literature review is the panacea for successful research." Discuss.Present the pro (defines problem, reveals gap, guides method, prevents duplication) and the con (necessary not sufficient; analysis paralysis; GIGO; execution matters). Conclude it is a foundation, not the whole. 14. Describe how you would conduct a systematic literature search, including Boolean logic.Break the topic into concepts; gather synonyms/abbreviations/variants; set inclusion/exclusion criteria; choose databases (Scopus, IEEE, ACM, Springer, PubMed); use AND (narrow), OR (broaden), NOT (exclude); limit by subject/language/year; iterate and record sources. 15. Compare quantitative and qualitative research.Quantitative: numeric, experiments, pre-selected variables, controlled setting, statistical analysis, generalisation, answers "what". Qualitative: verbal descriptions, natural setting, emerging themes, holistic, researcher as instrument, purposive sampling, answers "why/how". 16. Explain the data-collection techniques used in qualitative research.Observation (outsider/participant; Hawthorne effect), Interviewing (types and strategies), Focus Groups, and Document Analysis (conversational, discourse, narrative, objective hermeneutic). 17. Explain triangulation and its role in research quality.Triangulation is using multiple data sources, subjects, methods and kinds of data (or combining quantitative and qualitative methods) to enhance validity and reliability and confirm that findings are not due to chance. 18. Describe the process of qualitative data analysis.Reading/immersion -> coding (a priori/grounded) -> displaying data -> questioning & verification -> data reduction -> interpretation. Classic three steps: data reduction, data display, conclusion drawing & verification. 19. Explain any four types of qualitative research.Case study (intrinsic/instrumental/multiple), Ethnography, Grounded theory, Action research (plan-act-reflect), Design-based research. 20. Explain the four levels of measurement with examples.Nominal (gender), Ordinal (income level/Likert), Interval (temperature - arbitrary zero), Ratio (weight/age - true zero). 21. Distinguish descriptive from inferential statistics and explain Type I and Type II errors.Descriptive summarises a variable (mean/median/mode/SD); inferential infers population characteristics from a sample. Type I (alpha) = rejecting a true null; Type II (beta) = failing to reject a false null. 22. Differentiate parametric and non-parametric tests and give examples.Parametric assumes normality and interval/ratio data (t-test, ANOVA, Pearson, regression). Non-parametric makes no normality assumption and suits nominal/ordinal data (Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon, Kruskal-Wallis, Chi-square, Spearman). 23. For a study comparing cholesterol levels of an exercise group and a diet group, state the IV, DV, H0 and the appropriate test.DV = cholesterol concentration (ratio); IV = treatment group (diet vs exercise). H0: there is no statistically significant difference in mean cholesterol between the two groups. Test: independent-samples t-test (after checking normality and homogeneity of variance via Levene's test). 24. What is mixed-methods research? When and how is it used?It collects and analyses both qualitative and quantitative data, integrates them (concurrent/sequential/embedded) and assigns priority. Used when one source is insufficient, to explain results, generalise exploratory findings, or enhance a study. Designs: convergent parallel, embedded, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential. 25. Of what relevance are formal methods in Computer Science research?They provide precision/unambiguity, enable validation and verification, support proofs of correctness/security/trustworthiness, detect errors early, and offer rigorous evaluation via model checking, theorem proving and algorithm analysis. 26. Explain the concepts of secondary data, experience survey, pilot study and case study.See Part B Q5b - give a one-line definition plus key characteristics for each. 27. Define a thesis and describe the structure of an MSc thesis.A written work resulting from original research submitted for a higher degree. Structure: initial pages (title, declaration, acknowledgements, abstract, contents, lists) + Ch1 Introduction, Ch2 Literature Review, Ch3 Methodology, Ch4 Results & Analysis, Ch5 Conclusions & Recommendations, References, Appendices. 28. What is an abstract? State its word limits and common mistakes.A brief summary of the entire thesis containing no new information; <=1 page A4; 150 words (MSc) / 350 (PhD); written last; covers problem, purpose, methods, results, conclusion. Mistakes: too much background/methods, references, abbreviations.
