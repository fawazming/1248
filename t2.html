QUESTION 4

(a) Characteristics of a good Research Problem

  • Interesting - keeps the researcher motivated throughout the study.
  • Researchable - can be investigated through collection and analysis of data.
  • Significant - contributes to the improvement and understanding of theory and practice.
  • Manageable - fits the researcher's skills, resources and time restrictions.
  • Ethical - does not embarrass or harm participants.
  • Also: clear and specific (not too broad/narrow), novel, feasible, and relevant (FINER).
A proper problem formulation and associated objectives make the work coherent.

(b) Key components of the following types of research

i. Quantitative Research

  • Uses numeric/numerical data and measurements, expressed quantitatively.
  • Tests hypotheses; variables (IV/DV) measured on defined levels of measurement.
  • Structured instruments (surveys/questionnaires); random sampling; controlled conditions.
  • Analysed with statistical methods (descriptive & inferential).
  • Aim: generalisation, objectivity, prediction.

ii. Qualitative Research

  • Uses rich verbal descriptions (words, not numbers); "thick" description.
  • Natural setting; researcher immersed; assumes multiple realities.
  • Data collection: observation, interviews, focus groups, document analysis.
  • Purposive sampling; analysis by coding and themes; emergent/flexible design.
  • Aim: understanding of meaning (why/how), not frequency.

iii. Mixed-Method / Mixed Research

  • Combines both qualitative and quantitative data collection and analysis.
  • Integrates/links the two (concurrent, sequential, or embedded); assigns priority.
  • Designs: convergent parallel, embedded, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential.
  • One method's strengths complement the weaknesses of the other; stronger evidence and triangulation.

iv. Applied Research

  • Aimed at solving a specific, practical/real-world problem.
  • Uses and applies existing knowledge/theory rather than generating new theory (contrast: basic research).
  • Requires a clear practical problem, a defined context, and a solution/innovation.
  • Outcome is applied/practical - a product, technique or recommendation of immediate relevance.

QUESTION 5

(a) Relevance of Formal Methods in Computer Science research

  • Precision and unambiguity - logic/mathematics describe the model exactly, removing ambiguity of informal description.
  • Validation and verification - validation checks that the specification truly specifies the problem; verification checks that the implementation follows the specification.
  • Enables proof of properties such as accuracy, security, trustworthiness, correctness, reliability and consistency.
  • Supports early detection of errors, reducing risk in safety- and security-critical systems.
  • Provides rigorous evaluation via model checking, theorem proving and algorithm (complexity) analysis.
  • Useful for protocols (e-voting, authentication), information systems, ATM and device APIs.

(b) Explain the characteristics of the following concepts

i. Secondary data - data that already exist and were collected by someone else / for another purpose (e.g. government reports, censuses, health records, published datasets). Characteristics: economical, time-saving, readily available, often large-scale/longitudinal; but may be outdated, incomplete, not tailored to your question, and of variable quality - so reliability, accuracy and supplier must be checked.

ii. Experience survey - a preliminary/exploratory investigation that gathers the insights and opinions of experienced/knowledgeable people (experts, practitioners) to understand the problem area, identify variables and refine the research problem/objectives. It is qualitative and exploratory, relying on expert judgement, and is used at the problem-formulation stage.

iii. Pilot studies - a small-scale, preliminary trial/rehearsal of the main study conducted before the full research. It tests and refines the instruments, sampling and procedures, checks validity and reliability, estimates time/cost, and exposes flaws before the main study. Characteristics: miniature version, small sample, not meant to test the main hypothesis, used to improve the design.

iv. Case studies - an in-depth study of a single case (individual, group, organisation, event or system) in its real-life context, especially when the boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clear. Focus is on the individual case; data may be quantitative or qualitative; a case may be simple or complex. Types: intrinsic (the case itself is of interest), instrumental (case studied to provide insight/refine theory), and multiple (several cases studied jointly).

QUESTION 6

(a) Define the term Thesis

A thesis is a written work resulting from original research, especially one submitted for a higher degree in a university. It demonstrates an understanding of the state of the art, a critical appreciation of existing work, and a novel contribution, which is systematically evaluated.

(b) Structure of your proposed M.Sc Research Thesis

Initial pages: Title page -> Declaration -> Acknowledgements -> Abstract (and translation) -> Table of Contents -> List of Tables -> List of Illustrations -> List of Symbols/Abbreviations.

  1. Chapter 1 - Introduction: background, statement of the problem, aim and objectives, research questions/hypotheses, significance, scope and limitation, overview of method.
  2. Chapter 2 - Literature Review: DL, FL, XAI and IoMT concepts; related works; identification of the research gap.
  3. Chapter 3 - Research Methodology: DSR approach, the six-layer framework (data acquisition -> edge preprocessing -> local deep learning -> federated aggregation -> XAI layer -> clinician dashboard), datasets, tools, metrics, experimental setup.
  4. Chapter 4 - Results and Discussion: results on accuracy, privacy, explainability and communication efficiency; comparison with baselines; interpretation and links to literature.
  5. Chapter 5 - Conclusions and Recommendations: summary of findings, contributions, limitations, and future work.

End matter: References (UKM style) and Appendices.
