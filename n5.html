Question 3 | Intelligence in Artificial Intelligence

a) AI vs AI Research vs Artificial Intelligence System

TermMeaningExample / Note
Artificial Intelligence (AI)The broad field / science concerned with building machines that display intelligent behaviour - the umbrella discipline. It includes ideas, theory, tools and goals.The whole subject of making machines intelligent.
AI ResearchThe systematic scientific activity / study within computer science that investigates and creates intelligent agents - an agent that perceives its environment and acts to maximise its goal achievement.The R and D process: studying, experimenting, publishing.
Artificial Intelligence SystemThe concrete artifact / implementation (software or hardware product) that results from AI research and performs an intelligent task.A chatbot, expert system, chess engine, self-driving car.

Key Idea: AI = the field; AI Research = the activity/study; AI System = the deployed product. Field -> study -> artifact.

b) Five types of human intelligence AI might replicate

Human intelligence is often described by Gardner's multiple intelligences. Five that AI increasingly attempts to replicate:

  • Linguistic intelligence - using language (speech recognition, translation, chatbots).
  • Logical-mathematical intelligence - reasoning, calculation, theorem proving, game playing.
  • Spatial intelligence - perceiving and manipulating space (computer vision, robotics, navigation).
  • Musical intelligence - recognising and producing music / pitch / rhythm.
  • Interpersonal (social) intelligence - understanding people, emotions and social interaction (affective computing, social robots).

Other types include bodily-kinaesthetic (motor skills / robotics) and intrapersonal (self-awareness). The lecture list also groups intelligence as learning, reasoning, problem-solving and creativity, social behaviour, and sensory experience (hearing, sight, touch, taste, smell).

Memory Aid: Gardner's five quick recall: Word, Number, Space, Music, People.

c) Five activities that demonstrate human intelligence

  • Learning - acquiring new knowledge or skills from experience and adapting behaviour; e.g. a child learning to read, or an AI model trained on data.
  • Reasoning - drawing logical conclusions or inferences from facts and rules; e.g. diagnosing an illness from symptoms, or solving a logic puzzle.
  • Problem solving - finding a strategy / sequence of actions to move from a starting situation to a desired goal; e.g. planning a route or playing chess.
  • Creativity - generating novel and useful ideas, designs or works; e.g. writing a poem, composing music, designing a machine.
  • Communication / language use - understanding and producing language to convey meaning; e.g. conversation, reading, translation.
  • Perception and social behaviour (also acceptable) - sensing the environment (sight, hearing, touch) and interacting appropriately with others.

d) Strong AI vs Weak AI

AspectWeak AI (Narrow AI)Strong AI (AGI)
ScopePerforms one specific, narrow task.General intelligence across many/all tasks.
UnderstandingSimulates intelligence; no real understanding or consciousness.Would genuinely understand, reason and be self-aware.
ExamplesSiri/Alexa, face recognition, chess engine, spam filter, self-driving car.Still theoretical - a machine with a mind, e.g. human-like all-round thinking.
Status todayExists and is widely deployed.Not yet achieved; long-term research goal.

Key Idea: Weak = specialised tool that acts intelligent. Strong = general machine that is intelligent (mind/consciousness). Most AI today is weak/narrow.
