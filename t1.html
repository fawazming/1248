QUESTION 1

(i) State the Research Problems / Research Gaps (at least two paragraphs)

Gap 1 - Privacy vs. transparency in IoMT deep learning. Existing Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) disease-prediction systems rely on centralised deep learning, which achieves high accuracy but requires patient data to be pooled, exposing sensitive records to breach and regulatory risk (HIPAA, GDPR). Federated Learning (FL) removes this risk by training locally and sharing only model parameters; however, the resulting models are opaque "black boxes", so clinicians cannot see why a prediction was made. This limits trust and clinical adoption.

Gap 2 - Absence of a jointly evaluated framework. Although Explainable AI (XAI) methods such as SHAP and LIME have been applied to healthcare models, and some recent works combine FL with XAI for IoMT, evaluation has been narrow - one or two dimensions at a time. No existing framework jointly evaluates accuracy, privacy preservation, explainability and communication efficiency in one standardised protocol. This absence of a jointly evaluated, explainable, privacy-preserving framework is the research gap this study addresses.

(ii) Research Title

Design and Evaluation of an Explainable Federated Deep Learning Framework for Privacy-Preserving Disease Prediction in Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) Healthcare Systems.
Rule: be specific & accurate; avoid phrases like "A study of..." and avoid unnecessary jargon/acronyms in the title.

(iii) Research Objectives

Aim: To design, implement and evaluate an Explainable Federated Deep Learning framework that enhances patient privacy while improving transparency, trust and predictive performance in IoMT healthcare systems.
  1. To review DL, FL and XAI techniques for IoMT healthcare applications.
  2. To identify the limitations of existing privacy-preserving healthcare AI models.
  3. To design a framework integrating FL, DL and XAI for IoMT.
  4. To implement the framework using selected public healthcare datasets.
  5. To evaluate the framework on accuracy, privacy, explainability and efficiency.
  6. To compare the framework against existing healthcare DL models.
Objectives use action verbs and are SMART.

(iv) Research Questions (at least five)

  1. How can Federated Learning improve privacy preservation in IoMT healthcare systems?
  2. How can Explainable AI improve clinicians' understanding and trust in AI predictions?
  3. Can FL and XAI be integrated to improve both privacy and transparency without significantly reducing accuracy?
  4. How does the proposed framework compare with existing healthcare AI models in accuracy, privacy and explainability?
  5. How does the framework perform in terms of communication cost and training time across multiple hospital clients?

(v) Research Methodology (with workflow diagram)

Design:Design Science Research (DSR) - iterative framework design, implementation and experimental evaluation. Data: public datasets (UCI Heart Disease, PIMA Indians Diabetes, MIMIC-IV, PhysioNet ECG, COVID Chest X-ray). Tools: Python, TensorFlow/PyTorch, Flower (FL simulation), FedAvg, SHAP & LIME. Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC; communication cost & training time; privacy-preservation score; explanation quality (SHAP/LIME fidelity).

Problem Identification -> Framework Design -> Framework Implementation -> Experimental Evaluation -> Performance Analysis

Draw a feedback arrow from Performance Analysis back to Framework Design (iterative DSR). The six-layer architecture is: IoMT Data Acquisition -> Local Edge Preprocessing -> Local Deep Learning -> Federated Aggregation (FedAvg) -> Explainable AI Layer (SHAP/LIME) -> Clinician Dashboard.

QUESTION 2

(a) Stages of the Research Process (annotated diagram)

Four activity stages: (1) Determine the type of research -> (2) Plan how to do it -> (3) Do the research -> (4) Deliver the output.

Literature Review -> Problem Statement -> Research Objectives -> Research Design -> Research Methodology -> Research Activity -> Research Result -> Conclusions

Annotation: Literature Review through Research Methodology = the research proposal; Research Activity to Conclusions = the research execution. The process is iterative (feedback loops and refinement).

(b) Five activities mistaken for research

  1. Mere information gathering / discovery or transportation of facts - collecting and moving facts without interpreting them; misses the essence of research.
  2. Preparation of teaching materials - routine compilation, no attempt at new knowledge.
  3. Provision of advice or opinion (consulting work) - expert opinion given to a client, not a systematic inquiry.
  4. Routine data collection (and routine information-system development) - no focused question, no experimental design, no new knowledge; e.g. routine system development that is not an experimental design of a new product/service.
  5. Playing with technology / book report / programming project / doing what others have already done - no originality or contribution.
Note: each can still contribute to a research endeavour when done within a focused question, method and evaluation.

QUESTION 3

(a) What is a Literature Review?

A literature review is a systematic, comprehensive and critical review that identifies, evaluates and interprets previously published work (by researchers, scholars and practitioners) that is related to your topic. It appears as a chapter in a dissertation or as the theoretical background of a paper, increases the importance of your topic, and is an interpretation and synthesis of existing knowledge that culminates in identifying the research gap.

(b) "Intensive Literature Review is the panacea to the successful completion of Research work." Juxtapose.

In support (pro):

  • It defines and limits the problem and prevents unnecessary duplication.
  • It reveals the research gap and justifies the study, sharpening objectives and questions.
  • It places the work in historical perspective and evaluates promising methods.
  • It prevents wasting time on dead/dying topics and supplies the theoretical framework.

Against (con):

  • It is necessary but not sufficient - success also needs sound problem formulation, rigorous methodology, correct data collection/analysis and interpretation.
  • Over-reliance can cause "analysis paralysis" and delay, especially for a time-bound Master's.
  • The quality of the review depends on the quality of the sources - garbage in, garbage out.
  • Only research execution and contribution (not reading) complete the work; a review does not generate findings by itself.

Verdict: An intensive literature review is indispensable and highly enabling, but not sufficient on its own - it is the foundation, not the whole building.
